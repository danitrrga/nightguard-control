## Decisions Made

- **`pick()` validates with `int(value[1:], 16)`, not `textual.color.Color.parse`.** The
  threat register (T-12.1-05) requires each candidate to be a parseable colour, not merely
  `#`-prefixed, so a length check alone was not enough — `#zzzzzz` would have passed it and
  blown up later inside Textual's colour system. `Color.parse` would be the most faithful
  check, but `theme.py`'s module docstring states "imports only `tomllib` (stdlib) and
  `textual.theme.Theme` so the loader and watcher are unit-testable headless", and that
  claim has consumers. A stdlib hex parse satisfies the threat and keeps the import set.
  Length gate stays `(4, 7, 9)` — `theme.py`'s existing house idiom at `palette()` — which
  also admits the `#RRGGBBAA` form that `panel.py`'s `_rgb` would reject.
- **`panel`'s chain tail is the resolved `surface` value, not a key lookup.** The contract
  writes the chain as `lighter_background → selection → surface`, and `surface` is not a key
  any installed theme declares. Implemented as `pick("lighter_background", "selection",
  "surface") or surface` — it honours both readings and cannot leave `panel` unset.
- **`primary`, `background`, `foreground` and `accent` keep direct `c[...]` reads.** The
  `KeyError` they raise is the mechanism routing a partial `colors.toml` to the
  `alacritty.toml` fallback in `load_omarchy_theme`. A chain there would swallow it and
  break `test_malformed_alacritty_fallback_raises_for_app_handler`.
- **The unreached rungs stay, and the docstring says why.** `urgent`, `orange`, `selection`
  and `dark_background` were reached by none of the 27 themes. They are named in the
  docstring as insurance for a theme not on this box, specifically so a later reader does
  not delete them as uncovered dead code.

**3. [Rule 2 - Missing critical functionality] `pick()` parses rather than pattern-matching**

- **Found during:** Task 1
- **Issue:** the nearest house idiom (`palette()`, `theme.py:191`) validates only
  `startswith("#")` and length. T-12.1-05 in this plan's own threat register requires
  "a `#`-prefixed **parseable** colour". A malformed value such as `#zzzzzz` passes the
  length gate and reaches `Theme(...)`, which is a plain dataclass and does not validate —
  so it would surface as a crash later, in the colour system, far from the theme file.
- **Fix:** `int(value[1:], 16)` inside a `try` in `pick()`. A malformed candidate is skipped
  and the chain continues, so the role degrades by one colour rather than the palette dying.
- **Files modified:** `ngtui/ngtui/theme.py`
- **Committed in:** `eaa501d`

## Issues Encountered

**A regression in the `surface` or `panel` chain would be caught by neither assertion, and
this plan deliberately did not close that.** Distinctness covers only the three verdict
roles. Provenance cannot see an in-theme fallback (measured above). So reverting `surface` to
`color0`-only — which would put `surface` back on top of `background` for the 25 themes
declaring no `color0` — leaves control 4 fully green.

The assertion that would catch it is an *aggregate* one: "at least N of the installed themes
resolve `surface` distinct from `background`" (23 of 27 under the fix; 2 of 27 under a
`color0`-only revert). That is true, non-vacuous, and does not make the per-theme claim D-11
forbids. It was **not** added, for two reasons that are the phase's call rather than an
executor's: D-11 argues against surface-vs-background comparison as a class, and the plan's
acceptance criterion greps the file for `surface.*!=.*background` expecting zero — a count
the aggregate form could only satisfy by writing the operands in the other order, which is
dodging the check rather than passing it. Flagged here for a later plan to decide.

Second, smaller: `Theme.panel` is now populated and nothing reads it. Plans 03–04 wire the
control ladder; if `panel` stays unread through the phase, it is a field set for a consumer
that never arrived, and the honest move then is to drop it from the chain list rather than
leave a role maintained on spec alone.
