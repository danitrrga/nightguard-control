E1★ · keyline grid, modernized

The no-boxes grid with the reference's mechanisms actually applied: a hairline device frame bracketing the viewport with small dim corner labels instead of headings, a row of square outline chips for the actions with the one primary filled, and the day promoted to full-width hero rendered as an ASCII density ramp. Colour budget: the ramp is greyscale because density is already carrying the information, so the entire budget goes to one subject — the verdict — plus one accent dot. Accent coverage is now under 1%, where before it was whole red bands.

E3★ · solid chrome, modernized

Same frame and same hero. The six filled title bars are gone — they were six saturated strips on one screen, which is exactly the guardrail the reference warns about. What replaces them: corner labels for the quiet regions, an inverted chip for the one region that is shouting, and a solid spine on the state region. Bold Digits, and the enforcement state as a reverse-video tag rather than a coloured word. Keeps E3's "this is software, not a terminal" read while spending a fifth of the ink.

The ramp, on its own

The hero, at four widths and in every state, so it can be judged as an instrument rather than as texture. Density is hours-until-curfew: inside the curfew it saturates to @, the moment it lifts it drops to ., and it climbs back through : - = + * # % as the night approaches — so you can feel 21:30 coming at 17:00 without reading a number. The thin underline is the window in which the curfew may be weakened at all. One bright cell is now. This is the one thing on the screen that is genuinely ASCII art, and it is the product's own output — which is the reference's actual rule: one hero material, and it should be what the tool makes.
