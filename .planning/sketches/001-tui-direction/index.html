Sketch 001 · direction

A · Panel stack

The bar panel, grown into a window. Literally the Quattro kit: a hero (icon + bold title + outlined detail pill + uppercase dim caption), then PanelSectionHeader / PanelSeparator / rows, all the way down. Axis: nothing is invented — if the shell has a component for it, this uses it. Cheapest to build, and the strongest coherence claim. Risk: a list of rows is not "seeing the cycle".

B · Cycle dial

The day as a 24-hour ring, drawn on the character grid — the same idea as the dial already in the bar panel, but given the whole window. Curfew is an arc you can see the end of; now is a marker travelling it. Axis: time is spatial, not a number. Blocking and tokens ring the dial as satellites. Risk: one big ornament that has to keep earning its 18 rows.

C · Two-column console

135 columns is a wide window; this is the only variant that uses them. Left column is the pact — verdict, cycle, what it costs. Right column is enforcement — what is actually being blocked right now, and what is not. Axis: blocking gets equal billing with the curfew, not a footnote. Risk: two columns is two ideas per screen.

D · Day band

The 24 hours laid flat across the window: curfew shaded, the edit window marked, now a bright cursor on the line. Reads at a glance like a progress surface in a normal app, and it is the only shape that shows curfew and the edit window in the same picture — which is the thing that is genuinely hard to hold in your head. Axis: one horizontal instrument, stats hung underneath.

E · Tile grid

Quattro control surfaces as tiles — each one a fill at 4% over the foreground with a 30% border, exactly what every button and field in the shell is made of. Verdict and blocking get the big tiles. Axis: the screen is a dashboard of objects, and blocking is one of the two biggest. Risk: the slop catalogue's "card containers around things that are not cards" — a tile has to mean an object.
